# Outline v0 (≈ 8–10 min)

1. **Cold open (0:00–0:40):** "A Stanford study says AI can tell liberals
   from conservatives with 72% accuracy. Could a CCTV camera tell your
   politics?"
2. **Who is Kosinski (0:40–1:30):** digital-footprints work, then faces;
   his framing as a privacy warning.
3. **The 2021 study (1:30–3:00):** 1 million photos; what "72%" actually
   means (pairwise AUC). Animation: two faces side by side vs one face alone.
4. **The 2024 controlled study (3:00–4:30):** lab photos, r = .22, ~5% of
   variance; politicians r = .13.
5. **One person, one photo (4:30–5:45):** chart from E1: 55–66% vs a 50%
   coin flip. A 20-photo "test" swings from useless to impressive by chance.
6. **What the model is really seeing (5:45–7:00):** grooming, expression,
   camera angle; "Physiognomy's New Clothes"; the 2018 sexual-orientation
   controversy.
7. **CCTV & security (7:00–8:30):** politics ≠ threat; base-rate chart:
   ~4,100 innocent people flagged per real threat; CCTV image quality;
   EU AI Act ban; behaviour-based alternatives (crowd density, abandoned
   objects).
8. **Close (8:30–9:30):** the real lesson is privacy. Faces leak a little
   information at scale, which is dangerous for populations and useless for
   judging individuals.

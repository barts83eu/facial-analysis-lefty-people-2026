Explore what those effect sizes imply under a simple simulation — and how much uncertainty remains.

02 Reading the numbers

A group effect
leaves uncertainty.

A correlation of .22 does not mean 22% accuracy. Squaring it gives about 5% of variance explained. The simulation then models how overlapping score distributions affect a binary decision.

Even in this balanced scenario, the lab-photo estimate produces about 41 incorrect calls per 100. It does not establish a person's politics from their photo.

Simulation method and assumptions

The project assumes two equally common groups with normally distributed scores and equal variance. It converts r to d using d = 2r / √(1 − r²), then accuracy = Φ(d / 2). For AUC, d = √2 × Φ⁻¹(AUC). Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function.

The 100 cells illustrate rounded expected counts; they are not observations, identities, or individual predictions. These model assumptions do not validate use on new populations or CCTV footage.

03 Interpreting the signal

What a photo
contains

The project's review distinguishes facial structure from presentation, demographics and photographic conditions. A model can use all of them.

01 / Presentation

Head pose

The 2021 study reported differences in how directly people faced the camera.

02 / Presentation

Expression

Expression was associated with the signal in everyday photos. The lab study used neutral expressions.

03 / Presentation

Grooming

Hair, glasses, makeup and facial hair can carry information about self-presentation.

04 / Population

Demographics

Adding demographic variables raised the 2024 lab result from r = .22 to r = .31.

05 / Measurement

Photo conditions

Lighting, framing and platform style are part of an image. They are distinct from facial structure.

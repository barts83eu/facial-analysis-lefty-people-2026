# 03 — Critiques and confounds

## Confounds in naturalistic photos

| Confound | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Facial expression | Liberals smiled more / differently in some samples |
| Head pose & camera angle | Selfie angle changes apparent jaw/nose/forehead proportions [AgueraYArcas2018] |
| Grooming | Beards, glasses, makeup, hairstyle differ by subculture |
| Image properties | Lighting, filters, resolution, background, platform |
| Demographics | Age, gender, ethnicity correlate with politics and with appearance |
| Sampling | Dating-site / Facebook users who self-report politics ≠ population |

[Kosinski2024] controlled most of these and the effect shrank from AUC .72
(naturalistic) to r = .22 (≈ AUC .63).

## Main critiques

1. **"Physiognomy's New Clothes"** [AgueraYArcas2017]: places these
   methods in the lineage of discredited physiognomy (e.g., Lombroso;
   [WuZhang2016] criminality-from-faces paper).
2. **Stereotype vs truth** [AgueraYArcas2018], [Peterson2022]: models
   can learn the *cultural markers* groups adopt, not innate traits.
3. **Causal story is speculative**: prenatal hormones (sexual orientation)
   or "facial morphology ↔ personality ↔ politics" (political orientation)
   are not directly tested.
4. **Ethics** (GLAAD/HRC, privacy scholars): publishing the method could
   enable persecution, especially where homosexuality or dissent is
   criminalised. Kosinski's counter-argument is that the risk exists anyway
   and the public and lawmakers need to know.

## Where Kosinski's own data limits his claims

- He reports the politicians' sample at r = .13, a weak result that is
  easy to miss next to the headline.
- His fWHR paper [Kosinski2017fWHR] is a null result against facial
  personality claims.

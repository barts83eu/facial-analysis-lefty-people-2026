Can a face reveal someone's politics?

A review of Michal Kosinski's Stanford research on predicting political orientation from facial images: what the studies found, what the numbers mean for one person in one photo, and whether any of it works for CCTV security.

PI: Bart Sakwerda Orchestration: Claude Opus 5.5 Updated 2026-10-25 中文版
Conclusion A weak signal across large groups, close to a coin flip for one person.

Faces carry a small statistical signal about political orientation. Applied to one person from one photo, the best published result is right about two times in three. The best-controlled result is right about 59% of the time. A coin flip is right 50% of the time.

CCTV and public-space security

Scenario: a crowd of 100,000 includes 10 people who intend violence. We give the face model the best published score (AUC .72), which no study has shown for violent intent, and set it to catch half of them.

Real threats flagged5

Half of the 10.

Innocent people flagged20,487

About 4,100 for every real threat. Precision 0.024%.

  • Politics isn't threat. The studies measure ordinary left/right self-identification. Violent extremists are a tiny minority on every side.
  • CCTV footage is worse. Every result used clear, frontal photos. No study shows the signal survives low resolution, angles or motion blur.
  • Law. The EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(g) bans inferring political opinions from biometrics. In the US: Illinois BIPA, Texas CUBI, California CPRA, FTC enforcement (Rite Aid, 2023), city bans.
  • What does have evidence: crowd-density and flow anomalies, abandoned objects, fight and fall detection, perimeter intrusion, and authorised post-incident search.

Cambridge Analytica and psychographic targeting

  • Origin. Kosinski, Stillwell and Graepel (2013) showed that Facebook Likes predict personality and politics. Cambridge Analytica first asked Stillwell for the myPersonality data; he refused, citing data protection. Aleksandr Kogan then collected data on up to 87 million users through his own quiz app (ICO 2018).
  • What the regulator found. The ICO's 2020 letter calls CA's methods "well recognised processes using commonly available technology" and says real-world accuracy on new people "was likely much lower". GSR data could not have been used in the Brexit referendum.
  • Does targeting work? Matz et al. 2017 reported up to 40% more clicks; Eckles et al. 2018 showed Facebook's ad delivery undermines that comparison. Tappin et al. 2023 found targeting on more than one attribute added nothing. Generative AI now writes personality-matched messages at scale (Simchon et al. 2024; Matz et al. 2024).
  • Data. Public: ICO, FTC and DCMS records. Not public: the harvested profiles (deletion ordered by the FTC, seized copies destroyed by the ICO) and myPersonality (sharing stopped in 2018). The project links to the official record only.

Who builds trait classifiers

The face models with documented deployment classify ethnicity, not politics. Reported cases: SenseTime (US Treasury 2021, Uyghur identification), a Huawei–Megvii "Uyghur alert" test (IPVM 2020), Uyghur detection by Hikvision, Dahua and Uniview (IPVM), and police procurement in 16 provinces (NYT 2019). Chinese university work includes Shanghai Jiao Tong (criminality, 2016), Dalian Minzu (ethnicity, Expression of Concern), and personality studies at the CAS Institute of Psychology, CAS-MPG Shanghai with Fudan, and NWPU Xi'an. Outside China: Stanford (Kosinski), the University of Southern Denmark, the Kachur group (Russia/UK) and Faception (Israel).

China's law treats biometrics as sensitive personal information (PIPL 2021, Art. 28–29). The 2025 face-recognition Measures exempt R&D and algorithm training (Art. 2(2)), so face-to-trait research falls only under the PIPL.

GitHub survey

No public repository reproduces Kosinski's face-to-politics pipeline, and he didn't release the images. Useful finds: One Million Impressions (how people judge faces, on synthetic faces), Chicago Face Database tools, and general face libraries (InsightFace, DeepFace). A few "physiognomy" apps make claims with no validation, which is good material for the video.
